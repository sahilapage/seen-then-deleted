Abstract

Driving world models are increasingly used to judge actions: a planner's candidate paths are rolled out in an imagined 4D future and vetoed if they collide. We test a minimal requirement for such a judge, that an agent it has just perceived does not disappear from the future it imagines. Three pretrained 4D occupancy world models from different families (camera-based trajectory-conditioned, latent diffusion, deterministic forecasting) fail it: on held-out nuScenes scenes, 59–79% of the moving vehicles they perceive are no longer covered by their imagined occupancy 3 s later, including vehicles that remain clearly visible. The deleted agents are the ones the model cannot forecast: for them, even an oracle choice among the model's own predicted blobs is placed correctly for only 12–17%, against 35–40% for the motion predicted by a camera detector.

Measuring this needs care: probability-weighted and any-cell permanence metrics are won by a blank uniform map or by scattered dots, so we propose a footprint-coverage versus false-area frontier that resists both. A training-free permanence layer that keeps every perceived agent as an object reduces deleted agents by 13.5, 15.5 and 6.6 points at equal false area in a pre-registered held-out test on all three models. For decisions, a physics safety check on the same detections, given the same at-fault rule, remains as good or better except at the rarest veto rates: permanence is necessary, not sufficient. Every main result survived two from-scratch re-implementations that import no analysis code, which also overturned several of our own earlier claims.

Seen, then deleted

A vehicle moving faster than 1 m/s within 40 m counts as perceived if the model's current map has mean vehicle probability of at least 0.3 in its box. It is kept if at least half of its true footprint 3 s later has probability of at least 0.2, and lost otherwise. On held-out scenes the frozen models lose most of the moving vehicles they perceive.

Two ways to delete

The deleted agents are the ones the model cannot forecast

If the model knew where a deleted car went but failed to keep it, its forecast should still hold the location. It does not, even when we give it every advantage: among all blobs of its +3 s forecast within the agent's reachable disc we pick the one closest to the truth. Per-cell occupancy has no notion of an agent, so a car that may be in many places becomes faint probability everywhere and, at any useful threshold, nothing anywhere.

A permanence measure that cannot be gamed

Two natural measures reward maps that carry no information. Summed probability (“false mass”) rewards spreading: a blank map with p = 0.004 everywhere loses no car at frozen SWTC's false mass. “Kept if any cell in the box is above τ” rewards sparse dots: a dot lattice around moving detections beats frozen SWTC by 19 points. We count a vehicle as kept only if its footprint is covered, and count as false alarm the area above τ farther than d from every true vehicle. Both axes are invariant to any monotone recalibration.

Fix: never let a perceived agent vanish

Since the deleted agents are those the world model cannot place, the fix must not rely on it to place them. The permanence layer keeps every moving vehicle that the camera detector (SparseDrive) reports now, places it at the detector's most likely motion at each horizon, renders its box into the imagined future and takes the cellwise maximum with the world model's map. It is training-free and needs no access to the model's internals. Both parts are needed: the detected boxes alone lose 73% of SWTC's perceived vehicles, more than the world model.

Learned alternatives also help. A transport readout trained on fold A, which fuses the forecast with the transported current map, gains 14.9, 13.2 and 3.9 points but needs the forecast. Fine-tuning SWTC on Occ3D targets gains 11 to 17 points on held-out drives, but retrains the world model and worsened its ranking of dangers. The layer fails where the detector misses an agent or mispredicts its motion; the failure case in the viewer above shows this.

Decisions: physics is a strong baseline

We compare the world model as a collision checker with physics (constant velocity on the same detections) at matched false-veto rates on real safe paths, with the label's at-fault rule (NAVSIM-style: ignore contact while stopped or from behind) applied to both checkers.

57–80%of real at-fault collisions caught by physics at 1–5% of safe paths vetoed. The world model: 51–78%.
0.25–0.5%veto rates are the only place the world model leads, and only on real collisions, not attacker ones.
84%of the attackers that SWTC deleted end more than 2 m from their constant-velocity path: a change of intent no checker foresees.

Adding the permanence layer to the verifier does not change this (A12, not supported). Our pre-registered A13 found a large gain from giving the world model part of a 1% budget, but it was registered against physics without the at-fault rule; with the rule on both sides the gain disappears. The detector misses the object that is hit in 9–30% of real and 33–54% of attacker collisions, where occupancy is the only safeguard. Permanence is necessary, not sufficient.

  1. Report permanence as footprint coverage against false area, not as summed probability or any-cell hits.
  2. Compare world-model verifiers with a physics check under the same at-fault rule.
  3. Carry perceived agents as objects, not only as cells.

Every registered test

Each confirmatory test was committed with the hashes of its code before its held-out outputs were computed. We list all of them, including those that failed or were superseded. Two from-scratch re-implementations that import no analysis code re-derived every number; the first found that our earlier axis, summed false mass, is won by a blank map, and we retracted the claims built on it.

Method

3 world-model families~3,000–3,500 perceived moving vehicles per model149 held-out scenes in two folds156 attack scenes

Models
SparseWorld-TC (SWTC): 200 × 200 × 16 occupancy at 0.4 m, +1/2/3 s from six cameras, conditioned on the ego path. DOME (latent diffusion) and the scene-centric forecasting branch of COME (deterministic UNet) receive four ground-truth Occ3D frames, so what they lose is lost by prediction alone. Official checkpoints.
Data
nuScenes validation, split by source scene into folds A (75) and B (74); everything is developed on fold A. Adv-nuSc adds 156 attack scenes for decisions, where Impromptu-VLA proposes 16 trajectories per keyframe.
Measure
Lost at threshold τ against false area(τ), the cells above τ farther than d from any true vehicle; methods are compared by lost at the frozen model's false area at τ = 0.2, with a scene-cluster bootstrap that re-matches the threshold in every resample. Collision checks are top-down, taking each voxel column's maximum vehicle probability over car height.
Layer
SparseDrive detections (score ≥ 0.3, ≥ 1 m/s; nuScenes-train checkpoint, run online), box along the detected velocity at the highest-scoring of six motion modes, rendered into each horizon and fused by a cellwise maximum.
Audits
Two from-scratch re-implementations rebuilt every result from raw nuScenes, Occ3D, model outputs and detector files. The 3D outputs shown here were regenerated and match the evaluated ones (top-down IoU 1.000).
